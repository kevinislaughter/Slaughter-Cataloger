What It Is

The Slaughter Cataloger is a single-file browser application for building a structured, exportable bibliography of periodicals — newsletters, magazines, journals, newspapers, zines, bulletins, and any other serialized publication issued in numbered installments.

It produces a canonical issue list: a record of what was published, when, and under what numbering — not a personal inventory of what you own. That distinction is intentional. Holdings tracking (which copies you have, their condition, what you paid) belongs somewhere else. This tool answers the prior question: what issues exist?

Everything runs locally in your browser. No account, no server, no data leaves your machine. The output is a standard CSV you can open in any spreadsheet application, adapt for Zotero or other cataloging tools. CSV is a deliberate choice for longevity: it is readable by every data tool that has ever existed and every one that will. As AI-assisted conversion becomes routine, a well-structured CSV can be transformed into virtually any target format — a different database schema, a custom bibliography system, a markup format that doesn't exist yet — with minimal effort. Fields that don't map cleanly to a new system's structure can be folded into a notes field automatically rather than lost. The data you enter today is not hostage to any particular piece of software, including this one.

What It Tracks

Fields are organized into two tiers:

Title Level — facts stable across the run of a publication: title, subtitle, former and later titles, publisher, editor(s), ISSN, language, series, city, country, frequency, and related identifying information. You fill these in once and they carry forward.

Issue Level — facts specific to a single numbered issue: volume, issue/no. within volume, whole number, year, month(s), day, date as printed, contributors, edition, printer, printer location, issue designation, price, pages, size, short name, and notes. Some of these auto-advance after each entry; others stay sticky until you change them.

The CSV schema is comprehensive: 45 columns covering everything from Series Number and Evidence Basis to Calendar Note and Supplement Label. It is designed to accommodate the full bibliographic range of serial publishing, including non-standard calendars, year-spanning issues, double issues, simultaneous editions, seasonal dating, and supplements.

Field States

Every field in the Cataloger can be in one of four states, and the interface makes all four visually distinct:

Filled — normal confidence. You've entered a value and stand behind it. The text appears in bold dark orange, distinct from labels and placeholder text.

Speculative. You've entered a value, but something is uncertain — the masthead is missing, the date was illegible, the information is secondhand, or what's printed is known to be wrong. Check the ? beside the field. The entry-wide Speculative toggle in the top right corner lights up automatically. In the exported CSV, both a boolean flag and a list of which specific fields were flagged are recorded, so the uncertainty travels with the data.

N/A. This field simply doesn't apply — the publication has no volume numbering, or this issue has no whole number, or ISSN was never assigned. Clicking N/A grays the field out and records it as explicitly addressed. This is meaningfully different from leaving a field blank: it says I considered this and it isn't applicable, not I haven't gotten to this yet.

Unaddressed. The field has not been touched — no value, no ?, no N/A. These fields show a light orange underline. This is a prompt, not an error. Many fields will legitimately stay unaddressed for a given issue, and that's fine. But when a field you'd expect to fill in is still orange after the rest of the form is done, it catches your eye. After you Record an entry and the form resets, any fields that cleared will briefly glow orange again — a gentle nudge to confirm or carry forward.

Two fields are pre-filled and sticky by default: Language (English) and Country (USA). For most English-language American periodical research these will never need to change. If you do change them — say, for a run of French literary journals — the new value sticks for the rest of the session.

Hover over any field label for a tooltip explaining what belongs there and how it differs from adjacent fields. The distinction between Issue / No. (within the current volume) and Whole No. (cumulative from issue one) is a common source of confusion, and the tooltips address it directly.

Key Features

Auto-advance. After recording an entry, Volume, Issue/No., Whole No., and Month(s) can advance automatically. The auto-advance panel lets you choose which fields participate. Month(s) advances by the span of what you selected — a bimonthly Jan–Feb selection advances to Mar–Apr — and wraps correctly at year boundaries, including year-spanning issues like Dec. 1989–Jan. 1990, where the year field updates to the end year on the next advance.

Season mode. For periodicals that date issues by season rather than month, toggle Month(s) to Season mode. Spring, Summer, Fall, and Winter work identically to months — multi-selectable, auto-advancing, sticky.

Issue Designation. The Special toggle marks an issue as a special or themed issue without displacing its normal numbering. Volume, Issue/No., and Whole No. remain fully editable; the designation (e.g. Special Poetry Issue, Double Issue, Anniversary Number) is stored and exported as a separate field alongside them. Supplement is handled distinctly: it marks an issue as appended to or issued alongside a numbered issue rather than as a numbered issue in its own right, which is a different bibliographic relationship.

Short Name. A compact reference label for each issue, auto-filled from the title plus volume/issue identifiers (e.g. The Saturday Review V2 I1). Intended for barcode labels and quick lookup. Override it freely — once you type in the field, auto-fill stops.

Examined / Reported. The top-right Examined toggle records the evidential basis of each entry. Examined means you have personally inspected this issue. Toggle to Reported and a source note field appears — record where the information comes from: a dealer catalog, WorldCat, a citation in another bibliography, a photograph from a colleague. This is not the same as Speculative: an entry can be confidently Reported (clear photo, trusted citation) or uncertainly Examined (damaged copy, illegible date). Both dimensions are exported.

Duplicate detection. The duplicate check uses Title + Series + Volume + Issue + Whole No. + Date as Printed + Edition, plus Issue Designation for special issues or Supplement Label for supplements. Two issues of different journals with the same numbering don't collide. Two editions of the same issue (City vs. National) are treated as distinct records.

Session persistence. Entries are saved reliably to browser localStorage on every change. If you close the tab accidentally, reopening the file offers to restore your previous session. A 15-minute CSV autosave runs as a secondary backup, though automatic downloads may depend on your browser's download permissions — manual CSV export remains the dependable way to make a permanent backup. Closing the tab with unsaved entries triggers a browser warning.

Undo delete. Deleting a row shows a six-second undo strip. One level, no drama.

Import / Export. Export to CSV at any time. Importing a CSV when entries already exist offers three choices: Replace, Append, or Cancel. Appended imports report how many possible duplicates were detected. The original cataloging order is restored from the Entry Order column, and the schema uses named columns, so future versions can add fields without scrambling existing data.

Two table views. The compiled issues table defaults to a compact view showing the most useful columns. Toggle to full view to see everything. Columns are sortable; the Entry Order column always lets you return to your original cataloging sequence.

A Note on Scope

This tool is designed for the full range of serial publishing — from mainstream magazines and newspapers to academic journals, trade publications, literary reviews, newsletters, and small-press periodicals with irregular publication history and idiosyncratic numbering. Fields like Former Title(s), Later Title(s), Alt. calendar, Supplement, and Issue Designation are not afterthoughts — they are there because the material demands them.

The Cataloger does not tell you how to catalog. It gives you a structure flexible enough to meet the material on its own terms.

Release History

Beta 1.1  —  July 8, 2026

New fields: Printer and Printer Location added at Issue Level — the printing house and its city are bibliographically distinct from the publisher and frequently change across a run. CSV schema updated to 45 columns.

Form: Edition moved before Printer in the Issue Level field order.

Duplicate detection: Key now includes Title and Series, so issues from different journals with the same volume/number no longer collide.

Table: Compiled issues table now has a fixed max-height (~7 rows visible) with sticky column headers and independent vertical scrolling, keeping horizontal scroll accessible. Compact view columns size to content rather than stretching to fill space. Compact view trimmed to: # · Short Name · Month(s) · Year.

N/A field behavior: Fields marked N/A no longer show the orange unaddressed underline. They now display [N/A] as placeholder text — confirming the field has been deliberately addressed. Original placeholder restores when N/A is toggled off.

N/A and Speculative are now mutually exclusive per field: activating one clears the other for the same field.

Speculative auto-toggle: the entry-wide Speculative flag now turns on automatically when any field is marked ? and turns off again when all field-level ? marks are cleared. If you click the Speculative header toggle manually, that choice is preserved until the next Record.

Supplement fix: Supplement mode no longer erases the base Issue / No. field. Volume, Issue, and Whole No. remain fully editable when Supplement is active; the supplement designation is stored separately in Supplement Label. Duplicate detection updated accordingly.

Sticky field policy clarified: Contributors, Edition, Day, Date as Printed, and Notes on Issue now clear after each Record. Printer and Printer Location are sticky. Labels updated to reflect actual behavior.

Cancel Edit now clears the form rather than leaving the edited row's data in place.

Import: replace, append, or cancel. Importing a CSV while entries exist now prompts with a three-way choice, and appended imports report how many possible duplicates were detected.

Short Name now updates correctly after auto-advance.

Stale Special Label is now cleared when Special mode is turned off.

Sort accessors added for all sortable columns; clicking any column header no longer risks a runtime error.

LocalStorage key versioned to slaughterCataloger.beta1_1.session to avoid collisions with earlier test versions.

Datalist values now use DOM methods rather than string concatenation, preventing edge cases with special characters in imported data.

Beta 1.0  —  July 6, 2026

Initial beta release (distributed as "Beta One"). Core data model established: two-tier form (Title Level / Issue Level), 43-column CSV schema, localStorage session persistence, duplicate detection, auto-advance, Examined/Reported evidence basis, Issue Designation, and full field-state system (Filled, Speculative, N/A, Unaddressed).
